The main risks that I identified with this project were electrical risks, rear axle risks, wiring risks, and braking risks.

Electrical risks: There were a few safety precautions I took to ensure that myself and others were safe at all times from short circuits, cell overheating, and BMS failure.

For short circuits, I made sure that metal tools stayed far away from the cells and wiring at all times unless I was intentionally using them for a specific task. I understood that the battery pack could easily start a fire due to a massive surge in electrical current through whatever was shorting the battery.

For cell overheating, the main thing was to make sure I was using a properly configured spot welder instead of a soldering iron to attach the nickel strips to the battery cells. A soldering iron could transfer too much heat into the cell terminals and potentially damage the cells.

I also made sure not to hold the soldering iron on the BMS for too long. It has sensitive MOSFETs and other components that could be damaged by excess heat. I needed to use proper soldering techniques, such as tinning, to make sure that I got strong solder joints quickly. The thicker-gauge wire I used for the P- and B- terminals was a bit harder to solder, so I needed to be patient and sometimes let the BMS cool down before trying again to get a proper joint.

I also made sure that the BMS had cell balancing so that the different cell groups would not drift too far apart in voltage over time. The BMS was also rated for 30 A of continuous current, which was more than enough for the current that the controller would draw from the battery.

Rear axle risks:

My main concern with the rear axle was that the added torque that the motor puts on the axle washers would wear down the rear drop outs and eventually cause the rear axle to become loose. To prevent this I installed a torque arm on the drive side of the rear axle that helps to distribute torque from those axle washers onto the arm and eventually into the chain stay bar. This helps to prevent rear axle failures that can end in a very bad crash.

Wiring risks:

My main concern with wiring is that one of the wires would get caught in the spokes of the wheels. To prevent this, I made sure to secure all wires, large and small to the frame of the bike using zip ties, and made sure that all connections were tight so that no wires were able to come loose.

Braking risks:

My main concern here is that the rim brakes will wear down the system overtime. This is a bit of an ongoing safety risk. The brake pads wear down the rim and outer tire due to high Ebike speeds and abrupt stopping, and this could lead to the wheel needing to be replaced. But more importantly, at high speeds, rim brakes do not provide as high of stopping power as disk brakes would. To address this issue, the best solution would be to install disk brakes that tend to have better stopping power and won't damage the wheels.
