The main risks that I identified with this project were electrical mistakes, rear axle failure, unsecured wiring, and brake failure.

**Electrical Mistakes:** There were a few safety precautions I took to ensure that myself and others were safe at all times from short circuits, cell overheating, and BMS failure.

For short circuits, I made sure that metal tools stayed far away from the cells and wiring at all times unless I was intentionally using them for a specific task. I understood that the battery pack could easily start a fire due to a massive surge in electrical current through whatever was shorting the battery.

For cell overheating, the main thing was to make sure I was using a properly configured spot welder instead of a soldering iron to attach the nickel strips to the battery cells. A soldering iron could transfer too much heat into the cell terminals and potentially damage the cells.

I also made sure not to hold the soldering iron on the BMS for too long. It has sensitive MOSFETs and other components that could be damaged by excess heat. I needed to use proper soldering techniques, such as tinning, to make sure that I got strong solder joints quickly. The thicker-gauge wire I used for the P- and B- terminals was a bit harder to solder, so I needed to be patient and sometimes let the BMS cool down before trying again to get a proper joint.

I also made sure that the BMS had cell balancing so that the different cell groups would not drift too far apart in voltage over time. The BMS was also rated for 30 A of continuous current, which was more than enough for the current that the controller would draw from the battery.
